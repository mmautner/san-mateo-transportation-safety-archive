# Transportation Safety
Community safety is a top concern for the City. Visit this hub to learn about the City’s ongoing work to make traveling in San Mateo safer and more comfortable for all ages and abilities. The City of San Mateo is committed to improving transportation safety for everyone, whether walking, biking, rolling, driving, or taking transit.
Here, you can find information about the City’s efforts to increase safety across our streets and how we build those priorities into our projects to make a safer San Mateo.
Through completed plans and ongoing capital projects, the City advances safety with targeted intersection improvements, school-area enhancements, bicycle network upgrades, and corridor-level investments.
We know transportation safety is a critical concern. Sign up for the [Transportation Safety email list](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/4707/Email-Alerts-News) to receive updates about this important work.

## Speed Limit Update
At the City Council meeting on Sept. 22, 2026, the Council introduced an ordinance that establishes enforceable speed limits for all roadway segments where the City’s Engineering and Traffic Survey recommended speeds stay at existing limits or were reduced from existing limits. Council deferred any action on segments where speeds were recommended to increase and asked staff to come back at a future study session to discuss those segments in greater detail.

Alameda de las Pulgas Corridor – Phased Safety Improvements
Staff are implementing a phased series of safety improvements along the Alameda de las Pulgas corridor, as follows:
- Immediate Term:
  - 31st Avenue Safe Routes to School Corridor Improvements: Traffic calming improvements are planned on 31st Avenue in front of Hillsdale High School, along with signalization and other intersection safety improvements at 31st & Alameda de las Pulgas. Design consultant selection is currently underway.
  - [Hillsdale Boulevard & Alameda de las Pulgas Intersection Safety Improvements:](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/4768/Hillsdale-Blvd-Alameda-de-las-Pulgas-Tra) This project will install a new traffic signal, protected bike lane facilities, and updated pedestrian ramps to enhance safety for all users and reduce congestion through the intersection. The project is currently out to bid, with award anticipated in mid-November and construction expected to be completed in 2027.
  - Alameda and Hobart Crosswalk Improvement [Quick-Build]: Signage and yield pavement markings will be installed at Alameda de las Pulgas & Hobart Street to improve existing crosswalk visibility. Estimated completion end of 2026.
  - Alameda and Aragon Analysis [Quick-Build]: The City is conducting an accelerated review of the Alameda de las Pulgas & Aragon Boulevard intersection to evaluate potential near-term enhancements. As part of this effort, staff is looking at options such as roadway markings, centerline delineation and signage that could be implemented within the Quick-Build program guidelines of under 6 months. Where existing traffic signal equipment allows, staff will also evaluate possible changes to signal operations that could separate pedestrian and vehicle movements at the intersection. The evaluation will be completed over the next 2 months, with installation completed within a 6-month period. Visit this website for updates as the project progresses.
  - Signalized Intersections North of Barneson Ave [Quick-Build]: The City is also reviewing other signalized intersections along Alameda de las Pulgas north of Barneson Avenue for potential changes, similar to those proposed at Alameda and Aragon described above. Following evaluation, the City would aim to deliver these improvements within the Quick-Build program guidelines of under 6 months. More extensive signal improvements, such as dedicated left turns or pedestrian signal heads, would be considered as part of future capital improvement projects rather than as part of the quick build program, if warranted
- Medium Term:
  - Alameda and Barneson Intersection Safety Improvement: An Intersection Multimodal Safety Improvement Project is planned at Barneson Avenue & Alameda de las Pulgas. This will include the installation of a new traffic signal and pedestrian-focused improvements. Funding for design has been requested for fiscal year 2027-2028 CIP budget.
  - Alameda and Hobart Crosswalk Improvement: This project will relocate the existing crosswalk approximately 30 feet north and install new curb ramps long with rectangular rapid flashing beacons (RRFBs). It is estimated to be completed in fall of 2027.
- Long Term:
  - Multimodal Complete Streets Project [Unfunded]: The corridor is identified in the City's adopted Bicycle Master Plan, shaped by significant community outreach, for bike facilities. The Project was ranked as top five priority in the County's Countywide Transportation Plan, reflecting its regional significance and supporting future funding eligibility.

## Intersection Safety Improvements
17th Avenue and El Camino Real Intersection Safety Improvements - Design
The City is working collaboratively with Caltrans, which owns and operates El Camino Real (State Route 82), to make the El Camino Real and 17th Avenue / Bovet Road intersection safer for everyone who walks, bikes, or drives through it. Improvements are being delivered in phases.

#### Completed: Exclusive Pedestrian Phase
On June 1, Caltrans reprogrammed the traffic signal to add an exclusive east-west pedestrian phase. This completely separates pedestrians from vehicles during the walk cycle, reducing conflicts. Drivers may experience longer wait times, particularly in the southbound direction on El Camino Real and the westbound direction on 17th Avenue.

#### In Progress: Second Phase of Safety Improvements
Caltrans is moving forward with additional improvements that build on the earlier upgrades:
- Easier-to-see signals: Reflective borders will be added to signal backplates on all approaches so drivers can spot the signals sooner.
- Clearer turns, safer crossings: Faded turn arrow markings will be refreshed, and a new "Yield to Pedestrian" sign will remind turning drivers to watch for people in the crosswalk.
- Better sightlines: About 230 feet of new red curb will be added on Bovet Road, and existing red curb will be refreshed, to keep sightlines clear on the intersection approach.
- Sharper lane guidance: New limit lines at all four approaches and yellow dotted extension lines for the left-turn lanes will help keep drivers positioned safely and predictably.
- Safer cycling: Bicycle boulevard markings on 17th Avenue will be refreshed so drivers and cyclists can better share the road.
These improvements are expected to be completed by the end of 2026.

#### Longer-Term Improvement
Protected left-turn signals will be installed for westbound and eastbound traffic on 17th Avenue and Bovet Road as part of Caltrans' planned State Highway Operations and Protection Program (SHOPP) project. The new signals will provide a dedicated left-turn phase that separates left-turning vehicles from opposing through traffic and pedestrian movements. This work requires more extensive construction and signal modifications. It is expected to support pedestrian safety while helping maintain traffic flow through the intersection.

#### Next Steps
The exclusive pedestrian phase signal timing will continue to be closely monitored following implementation. The City and Caltrans will continue to hold monthly coordination meetings to address safety concerns along El Camino Real.
Delaware Street and State Street Intersection Safety Improvements – Design
The City is advancing safety upgrades at the Delaware Street and State Street intersection to improve pedestrian visibility and shorten crossing distances.
Completed Immediate Improvements These quick-build improvements were installed by City maintenance staff:
- Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacons (RRFBs) at north and south crosswalks
- Shortened pedestrian crossings will be achieved by removing ~130 feet of curbside lane and associated parking spaces in the northbound direction and implementing paint and post curb extensions at the northwest and southwest corners of the intersection. This will reduce crossing distance by over 50%
- Upgraded intersection lighting for better nighttime visibility
Near-Term Improvements (9–12+ months) Permanent upgrades planned as a future capital project:
- Concrete curb extensions at north and south crosswalks
- High-visibility yellow crosswalks
- New ADA-compliant curb ramps
- Red curbs (no parking) near crosswalks to improve sightlines
Monte Diablo Avenue and San Mateo Drive Intersection Safety Improvements – Completed
Intersection traffic control has been converted from two-way stop control to all-way stop control, improving multimodal safety at the intersection.
Palm Avenue and 9th Avenue Intersection Safety Improvements – Completed
New safety improvements are now in place at the intersection of Palm Avenue and 9th Avenue. The project responds to community concerns about vehicle turning speeds, driver behavior, and reported near-misses between pedestrians and vehicles, especially for those accessing Central Park. Improvements include:
- New striping and flexible delineators that tighten the southwest corner, shorten the crosswalk, and encourage slower turns.
- Extended striping to the intersection to boost visibility and awareness of bicyclists.
- No reduction in the number of travel lanes.
- Removal of one on-street parking space on Palm Avenue to make room for the new safety features.
- Additional intersection upgrades are planned as part of the City's 2027 street paving project.

## Street Rehabilitation Supporting Safety
[Street Rehabilitation Supporting Safety webpage](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/2128/Paving-Our-Citys-Roadways)
The City’s pavement rehabilitation program improves roadway surface conditions, extends the life of streets, and enhances safety for all users. Resurfacing projects provide opportunities to incorporate bicycle and pedestrian improvements where feasible, including updated striping, high-visibility crosswalks, lane markings, and traffic calming elements. These coordinated rehabilitation efforts ensure that street maintenance not only preserves infrastructure but also contributes to safer travel for people walking, biking, and driving.
Transportation Capital Improvement Project Major Milestones
- [Peninsula Avenue & Delaware Street (construction expected early 2027):](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/4909/Peninsula-Ave-and-Delaware-St-Intersecti) A top 7 priority location in the City's Local Roadway Safety Plan, this project will add protected left-turn signals along Peninsula Avenue, and a new northbound right-turn signal to protect pedestrians crossing Peninsula Avenue. Additional improvements include a widened sidewalk on the southeast corner, curb extensions on Dwight to shorten crossing distances, reconstruction of three curb ramps for ADA compliance, and an accessible pedestrian signal system. The City of Burlingame is a design and funding partner on this project. The project is currently out to bid, with award anticipated in late December.
- [Humboldt Street Raised Crosswalks (construction expected early 2027):](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/4871/Humboldt-St-Bike-Lane-Alternatives)This project will install two raised crosswalks: one at Humboldt & College Avenue, and one mid-block between College Avenue and Peninsula Avenue at the Superior Courthouse (800 N Humboldt Street), to improve pedestrian safety and encourage slower vehicle speeds.
- [Humboldt Streetlighting Improvements:](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/4871/Humboldt-St-Bike-Lane-Alternatives) Replacement of cobrahead roadway luminaires is 100% complete along the full corridor extent, from Peninsula Avenue to 5th Avenue. Post-top pedestrian-scale lighting has been ordered and is anticipated to follow as a subsequent phase of this lighting improvement effort. The pedestrian scale lighting is expected to be installed in winter 2026.
101 Corridor Connect Mini-Grant Awards
The City of San Mateo received two awards totaling $1.7M through the 2026 101 Corridor Connect Mini-Grant Program, sponsored by SMCTA to advance bicycle and pedestrian safety projects along the Hillsdale Boulevard/US 101 corridor:
- Hillsdale Boulevard Complete Streets – $1,100,000 awarded (including SMCTA technical assistance) to advance conceptual complete streets design along Hillsdale Boulevard, including traffic signal optimization, curb extensions, safety improvements, and a Class II bike lane. The project will also analyze existing conditions along the corridor.
- Hillsdale Boulevard/US 101 Separated Bike/Ped Crossing – $600,000 awarded (including SMCTA technical assistance) to evaluate three crossing alternatives connecting Norfolk Street and Franklin Parkway: a new separated bike/ped bridge, repurposing the existing Hillsdale Boulevard bridge, or widening the existing crossing.
